Why Karakul

Lake Karakul lies inside an impact structure about 52 km across, at roughly 3,960 m in the Eastern Pamir. The Earth Impact Database gives its age as less than 5 million years. NASA's Curiosity rover explores Gale Crater on Mars, an impact crater about 154 km across. Both are impact structures in cold, dry settings, which makes Karakul a natural place to ask: how far does the similarity really go?

Scientific note. The Pamir is not identical to Mars or the Moon. This platform compares specific measurable parameters and states their limitations.

    How the index works

    ESI = Σ(wᵢ·sᵢ) / Σwᵢ over the parameters that have BOTH values; completeness = available / total

    This is an index of similarity for specific measurable parameters. It is not a validated habitat suitability score, and the Pamir is not identical to Mars or the Moon. A missing value is excluded from the sum and is never counted as 0.

    ParameterUnitRoleDefault weightHow it is compared
    • When two sources give the same parameter, the more direct one is used: measured, then satellite, then modelled, then calculated.
    • The limits (a 1000-fold pressure ratio, 100 K) and the default weights are choices made by the team. They are adjustable and are not derived from a validated model.
    • NASA POWER grid cells are about 55 km wide. In mountains one cell averages peaks and valleys, so site values are regional averages, not point measurements.
    • The Gale ground temperature stored now is a single-month average, not an annual mean.
